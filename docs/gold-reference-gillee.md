# WoW Forever Gold Making Reference

A combined reference built from Gillee's three-part "Complete Gold Guide" series, plus his launch profession-plan video `[Launch]`. **Travis is also playing a warrior**, and Gillee's launch plan is for a warrior main, so section 0 is the most directly actionable part. It's meant for Claude to consult when brainstorming gold-making ideas for WoW Forever, so it's organized by **topic**, not by video. Tags like `[Ep1]` show which video a point comes from.

## How to use this note (for Claude)

- **Context:** WoW Forever (Ep. 3 also calls it "Classic Plus") is a vanilla-based server with new systems. The videos were recorded **during beta, before release**, so treat item-specific claims as vanilla-era priors, not facts about Forever. Gillee says this repeatedly.
- **Gillee's philosophy:** he's a "dad gamer" with a full-time job who optimizes **gold per minute of in-game time**, not gold per hour. He makes money through professions and auction-house work while he's offline, and he dislikes active farming. Check whether that matches Travis's constraints before recommending anything.
- **Two rules that recur in every video:**
  1. **"The best gold farm is one you can do forever."** Pick a method you enjoy, or you'll burn out and quit.
  2. **"Do the math."** Know your cost to craft, the AH price after the 5% cut, the sale rate, and the deposit risk. Don't trust YouTuber gold/hour claims ("take like 30% off").
- **Forever-specific mechanics he mentions** (unverified, from beta): a legacy talent tree with gathering/crafting perks, a Black Market AH, two respec costs (legacy plus normal trees), waylaid crates, more quests, vellums (announced but not seen in beta), an enchanting "arcane" campfire perk, class-specific food, and a crackdown on RMT/boosting.

## 0. Gillee's warrior launch profession route `[Launch]`

**TL;DR route:** Skinning + Herbalism (1 to ~32-34) → drop Skinning, pick up **Alchemy** around the Whirlwind Axe quest → **Herb/Alch to 60** and build the nest egg → swap to whatever the best **raid profession** is at 60 (likely **Engineering** + something: Eng/Alch or Eng/BS).

### The plan, step by step
1. **Level 1: Skinning + Herbalism.**
   - Skinning is "too good to skip early": free money from what you already kill, and it's all vendorable, so it doesn't depend on a crowded launch AH. It has to be on the character doing the killing (you can't skin on an alt).
   - He picked Herbalism over Mining because it feeds **healing potions** all through leveling (very useful for a melee class) and feeds Alchemy later. He also says Forever ties herbalism into **First Aid** (unverified detail). He finds mining painful to keep leveled (hunting hills for nodes, balancing mining and smelting).
   - Send every herb you don't use to a **bank alt** and stockpile for Alchemy.
2. **Level ~32-34: swap Skinning → Alchemy** whenever you're next in town, timed to the **Whirlwind Axe quest**.
   - Solo, Alchemy makes the Cyclonian kill "free" with a **Nature Protection Potion**. With a group you can do the quest at ~30.
   - Turning a second gathering profession into a crafting one is a big power spike: your own elixirs and healing potions instead of buying them.
3. **Herb/Alch to 60.** No starting capital is needed because Herbalism feeds Alchemy directly.
   - **Speed is the edge:** fast levelers arrive at 60 with ~280-290 herbalism while most players are still profession-less. In Anniversary he had **near-zero competition in EPL and Winterspring for ~2 weeks**.
   - **Hold the herbs:** they're cheap early because nobody needs them yet, and prices **doubled or tripled within ~3-4 weeks** once the masses hit 60.
4. **At 60: build the nest egg, then swap to the raid profession.** Engineering will "likely be raid required." He might also go Eng/BS, or LW if a great recipe shows up.

### Alts for warrior conveniences (Forever lets level 1s craft)
- **Blacksmithing alt** (level 1 trained in Goldshire): mail it rough stone and it makes **sharpening stones**. It reaches ~75 BS just from your stone.
- **Tailoring alt** (same or another level 1 in Stormwind): mail it linen and it makes **bags**. In Forever bags can be crafted at level 1.
- **Enchanting/DE alt**, the same idea for your own enchanting mats.
- Getting there takes ~10 min: make a human, **death-skip** at the bandits outside Northshire, train in Goldshire/Stormwind. Horde equivalent: an orc to Orgrimmar via a death skip. The add-on he mentions is roughly "Logout Skips" (name not verified).
- This gets you stones and bags without carrying an expensive profession on your main. Even with one account you can do this on the same account.

### His Anniversary precedent (what he's trying to repeat)
- Bought all three flask recipes on his warrior, and they paid for themselves within ~3 weeks of launch. He sold flasks, Mongoose, healing and mana potions, "everything."
- Had a ~2,500g nest egg but ~1,000g tied up in flasks on the AH, so he **borrowed ~400g from friends** saving for epic mounts to buy the **Lionheart Helm** recipe. That recipe made him "amazingly rich." He expects Forever to have an equivalent: a BoE everyone wants, or a new flask or potion.

### Why Alchemy over armor crafting in Forever
- Blizzard seems to be making every profession worth having, so armor crafts may become **BoP perks** (e.g. a blacksmith-only BoP Lionheart) rather than sellable BoEs. That would hurt BS/LW/Tailoring as gold makers. **Speculative.**
- Alchemy demand is safe: devs (Aggrend) said they like a heavy-consumable raid scene, even with world buffs removed.
- Alchemy was reportedly the **least popular profession on beta** (beta was capped around level 30, so this is weak evidence).

### Pitfalls he warns about
- **Double gathering is overrated at launch.** Everyone gathers, so many herbs and ores trade at vendor price, and you end up vendoring or holding with no income. It also needs upkeep: let your skill fall behind your zone and it's wasted (e.g. arriving in Thousand Needles unable to mine iron, then Badlands mithril).
- **"No professions" works** (quests pay for skills, with enough left for a 40 mount), but skinning is free gold for very little time lost.
- **"Class professions" (warrior = Mining/BS) is the trap for warriors.** Blacksmithing eats huge amounts of ore. The 30-40 minutes spent farming for a piece of leveling gear would be better spent leveling, because low-level gear barely matters and quest greens get you to 60.
- **Don't take a combo you won't actually use.** Don't spend gold on professions at the expense of **class skills** (the 30-40 stretch is the expensive one).
- **Know the gathering skill needed for each zone's nodes before you leave it.** Example: 50 herbalism before Redridge/Loch Modan (Mageroyal) or before leaving Northern Barrens. Mining can be pushed with **smelting** (1 to ~110-115 mostly from smelting, by his recollection).
- **Levels 30-50 are the slog** where people quit or start alts. Plan the Alchemy swap and Whirlwind quest to land there as a power spike.
- Leveling gold trick: in STV, farm raptors for Jungle Stew and vendor it.

> "There's no right answer though, so just make a plan and stick to it."

He estimates **~60g/hr as a fresh 60**, depending on how Forever scales quest rewards. This is the main quick income for someone with two crafting professions.
- Dungeons with friends: roll for or split the epics.
- Level an alt that farms better than your main (warrior main plus mage alt is the classic combo) and give it two gathering professions.

## 2. The "Gilly Method": the 5-step sequence to wealth `[Ep2]`

1. **Get a character to max level.** Gold income, gray value, and farming efficiency are all better at 60.
2. **Build a nest egg** by farming.
3. **Use the nest egg as working capital** (crafting, recipes, flipping, buying mats for someone else to craft).
4. **Buy what you need only with the profit.** Don't dip below the nest egg, except for a true "smoking deal."
5. **Snowball:** repeat steps 3-4 until you have more gold than you know what to do with.

### Step 1: leveling tips that save or make gold
- **Don't stop at 40 to farm for your mount** (e.g. mages begging and selling ports in Orgrimmar). Questing at 43-44 earns more than that.
- **Skip skill ranks that don't matter.** Examples: higher Overpower and Mocking Blow ranks, nearly all Hunter tracking (except maybe humanoids), Paladin resistance auras, Curse of Recklessness past rank 1, and Heroic Strike past the early ranks. Ask yourself whether +5 damage is worth 2g.
- **Make a bank alt right away:** a level 1 parked in a capital city.
  - Mail every random green and "might need it" item to it, so you get unlimited mailbox storage. Use Return for instant retrieval. Mail sits 30 days, auto-returns, then sits another 30. This is how whales store investments.
  - It gives you AH access without your main traveling.
  - In vanilla any level could disenchant anything. If Forever keeps that, the bank alt can level enchanting and DE all your greens. Gillee expects a meta of level 1 alts using the enchanting "arcane" campfire perk (bonus DE mats), possibly located in Ratchet. **Unconfirmed.**

### Step 2: building the nest egg
- **Nest egg size:** "enough to craft whatever you're going to craft." Rough guide: **~500g**, or **the cost of an epic mount (~1,000g)**. If your money-maker is a 2,000g-mat item like Lionheart Helm, the nest egg needs to be 2,000g. If it's 45g flasks, 500g makes about 10 at a time.
- Good nest-egg sources: gathering (a sure thing, 100% profit), raw-gold farms, fishing plus cooking (now boosted, see below), enchanting for tips (needs no capital), and class services.
- **Don't flip to build the nest egg.** Flipping needs capital ("you need money to make money"). It's a snowball tool for later.

### Step 3: the nest egg as working capital
- **Volume vs value example:**
  - Option A: 100 potions at 10s profit each = 10g, and about 5 min of crafting (3-5 sec per craft). He thinks a 10s margin is about right for **Free Action Potions early on** (PvP demand at 60, not during beta).
  - Option B: 5 flasks at 2g profit each = 10g. Same profit for more capital and less crafting time.
  - In both cases, selling happens while you're AFK. Post in the morning, collect in the afternoon.
- **What makes a good craft:** (bottom line)
  1. **High sale rate is king.**
  2. **Profit margin after the 5% AH cut.** If you buy something at 10g, the AH price must be over 10.5g just to break even.
  3. **Low deposit cost.** The deposit scales with the item's **vendor** value and is lost if the item doesn't sell. Items that can't be vendored (enchanting mats, some quest items) have about zero deposit. Ideal: high AH value, low vendor value (e.g. Scroll of Strength).
- **Pre-selling** (great for expensive crafts like Hide of the Wild or Molten Helm): keep the mats in your bags, advertise the finished item in trade chat, and craft only when a buyer whispers. Mats sell faster than crafted items because they have many uses. If nobody bites, sell the mats back at a ~5% loss.
- **Spreadsheet everything.** Columns: mat costs (qty × price), cost to craft, AH price, **net profit = AH × 0.95 − cost**. Add sale rate if you can. He re-validates daily even while building his own tool.
- **Buy a gatekept recipe** with the nest egg. He spent his whole ~1,700g on a **Lionheart Helm** recipe in Anniversary and crafted roughly 600-800 of them at up to 200-300g profit each in phase 1. Other examples: Transmute Undeath to Water (daily cooldown), +4 Stats chest, Hide of the Wild, Flask recipes (Titans, Supreme Power).
  - **Payback math:** (recipe cost) / (profit per craft) = crafts needed. Example: a 500g flask recipe at 5g profit each needs 100 flasks, and he used to sell ~100 flasks/day on Nightslayer.
- **Fund someone else's craft:** buy the mats for a high-value item, have someone with the recipe craft it, **tip them well**, and sell it yourself. He made ~15 helms for one buyer this way.

### Steps 4-5: protect the principal and snowball
- Option 1 (wrong): spend all 900g on an epic mount, now at 0g, back to farming.
- Option 2 (right): 900g of crafts → +100 → 1,000 → +115 → 1,115 → +130 → 1,245 → ... → 2,000. Then buy the mount and keep the original 900g principal. "Passive income applied to WoW."
- The daily loop takes about 10 minutes: buy mats, craft, post. Spend leftover play time on whatever you enjoy (farming is optional).
- Endgame: once you have the mount, a month or two of consumes, and your alts, you keep running the loop anyway.

## 4. Enchanting deep dive `[Ep1][Ep2][Ep3]`

- In vanilla you can barely sell enchanting on the AH (only wands, oils, and DE mats), so income is **trade-chat spam with linked enchants, mats plus tip**. Tips range from a few gold to ~50g depending on the enchant and server maturity.
- "DE-scan the AH" money is real but small. **The big money is a rare recipe plus trade-chat spam.**
- **+4 All Stats chest** is the premier rare enchant (rare world drop, possibly Onyxia; only ~10-15 people had it per Anniversary server). Get it from a raid or guild drop if you can.
- **Treat enchanting like a gathering profession:** it needs no upfront gold because customers bring the mats, so it's a valid nest-egg builder.
- **Zero-deposit mats:** mats can't be vendored, so they have no deposit cost. Post 24h auctions every day slightly above market (e.g. 500 Greater Eternal Essences at 5.5g when the norm is 5g) and let the normal ups and downs fill them for free.
- **Vellums** (announced for Forever, not seen in beta): would allow AH sales of enchants. Deposit is likely ~0, but the 5% cut still applies, so trade chat may still win.
- **Tailoring + Enchanting factory:** craft cheap greens and DE them for a margin.

## 5. Alchemy deep dive `[Ep1][Ep2][Ep3]`

- Gillee's "sweet darling." Price the herbs, then craft in bulk while it's profitable.
- Profit comes from **volume**: a 10-50s margin × hundreds of potions. People dismiss small margins because they don't think about scale.
- **Demand timing:** single-character raiders buy consumables about an hour before raid.
- **Transmute Undeath to Water** (daily cooldown) turns near-worthless mats into valuable ones.
- **Proc math** (TBC+ alchemy and Forever's Master Chef style perks): each extra proc is worth the full sale value, since no extra mats are used, not just the margin. His example: a Gooey Spider Cake costs 1s82c to make and sells for 2s, but a proc nets ~3s82c-equivalent value.
- Key consumables to watch: Free Action Potions, Greater Fire/Frost/Nature Protection, Limited Invulnerability Potions (LIP), Mongoose, Mageblood, and flasks.

## 6. Fishing and cooking under the Forever legacy talent tree `[Ep2]`

- **Master Chef** perk: scales up to a **50% chance of an extra result** when cooking.
- The fishing perk gives a **50% chance of an extra fish**, so any vanilla fishing farm is worth about **+50%** (a 30g/hr farm becomes ~45g/hr). Example: Winter Squid in Azshara.

- Risk: low barrier to entry means fish prices can be supplied down to the floor.
- Ep3 also mentions a legacy perk that grants **essences once all your professions are maxed**, which could make leveling alts into an income source.

## 7. Flipping playbook `[Ep1][Ep2][Ep3]`

**Definition:** buy below normal price and repost soon at a higher price. You're profiting from temporary false supply or demand.

1. **Time arbitrage:** buy on off days and off hours (Friday/Saturday mornings, very early mornings, West Coast late nights) and sell into **raid nights** (Tue/Thu, ~7:00-8:30 PM ET). 24-hour auctions posted Monday afternoon or evening catch raid-day demand if you can't be online. Buy when supply is high, sell when demand is high. He's confident this works even on Forever mega-servers.
2. **Short-supply items:** recipes, epics, pets (Hyacinth Macaw, Sprite Darter/ooze pets), cosmetics. Panic-sellers list cheap, e.g. a Staff of Jordan bought and relisted for +100g. It's 10 minutes of effort, but the sale might take days or weeks.
3. **Early-server deferral ("varsity tactic"):** while everyone else saves for their 40/60 mounts, nobody buys anything. If you don't gather, skip the fast mount, put the ~1,000g into bars (copper, iron, mithril), and sell a month later at ~2x. Buy the mount then. Don't sit on idle gold.
4. **Market resets:** if something is far below its normal price (e.g. FAPs near vendor price, or copper bars at 2s when they normally sell for 3s), buy all of it and repost at normal.

**Hidden costs:** 5% AH cut, deposit and reposting costs, sale rate (a great deal that never sells ties up capital), and above all **KNOW THE PRICE**. You need historical price data to know what counts as a deal.

## 8. Investing playbook `[Ep1][Ep3]`

**The two drivers:**
1. **Inflation:** a 2-year-old server has far more gold than a 2-day-old one. Vanilla has few gold sinks (mounts, respecs, repairs). Forever adds sinks (Black Market AH, dual respec costs) **and** new sources (waylaid crates, more quests), so the net effect is unknown.
2. **Supply and demand shifts:** something useless today can be valuable in 2 months.

**What to look for:**
- **AH price near vendor value:** your downside is close to zero because you can always vendor it. Example: Aquamarine bought at ~11s (vendors for ~10s) was worth ~2g a year later. ×10,000 = ~20k gold. Bonus Forever risk and opportunity: Blizzard could add a new use for a junk mat (e.g. Pristine Leather going 10s → 2g).
- **Raid encounters that need a specific consumable:** Greater Fire Protection (elemental fire is hard to farm, so it spikes before MC, then crashes after the hype; "hype doesn't outlast the news"), Greater Frost Protection for Naxx, LIP (mats are cheap early and spike when LIP becomes relevant, maybe around the Dire Maul/PvP patch).
  - **Crowding risk:** if too many people invest, the stockpiled supply cancels out the demand spike.
- **Predictable shifts in player habits:** PvP is quiet while people level, so FAPs and their mats (Oily Blackmouth, Stranglekelp) are cheap. Demand rises with the honor/ranking system and stays useful in Naxx.
- **Phase-cycle timing:** player count is a left-skewed bell curve over each phase, and people take breaks around **2/3 of the way through a phase**. Buy raid consumables then, before the next phase is announced and players return.
- **Default rule:** "Rarely do useful items get cheaper in WoW because of inflation." Don't hold idle gold. Park it in consumables or enchanting mats, even short-term while saving for a mount.
- **Plaguebloom case study** `[Ep3]`: few uses early (Mongoose), then Mageblood plus longer raids (AQ, Naxx) plus heavy EPL competition in phase 6. **No dungeon in Forever drops it**, so it can't be farmed in instances. Price went from ~2g early to ~12g+ later. Lesson: check whether a mat can be **instance-farmed**. If it can't, supply is constrained.
- **Onyxia Scale Cloak** `[Ep3]`: scales are cheap at release while everyone farms Onyxia and get pricier as Ony stops being relevant. The cloak also needs a rare drop-only cloak component. Good long hold.

## 10. AH mechanics cheat sheet `[Ep2][Ep3]`

- **Faction AH cut is 5%.** Sell for 100g and you get 95g. (The neutral AH is different.)
- **Deposit:** based on the item's vendor value. It's refunded if the item sells and lost if it expires. High-vendor-value items (weapons, armor) are risky to repost. Non-vendorable items (enchanting mats) cost about nothing.
- **24h auctions** are underrated, especially on raid days and for zero-deposit items.
- **Mailbox as storage:** 30 days in the mail, auto-return, then 30 more days.
- **Sale rate** matters as much as margin. A smoking deal that never sells ties up capital.
